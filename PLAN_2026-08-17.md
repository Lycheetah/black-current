## PART 2 🔴 — THE FINDING: FOUR SURFACES CHANGE ONE SHIP

This is what *"the menu, the customize tool, and what else"* is circling, and it
is the most important thing in this document.

⛔ **THE ONLY FAILURE THAT MATTERS IS A SPLIT ROSTER.** There are now **four**
places that change your ship, and they disagree.

| # | surface | opened by | what it owns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **THE MENU** `js/garage.js` | title screen | callsign · **crew** · **hull style** · **skin/art pack** |
| 2 | **THE DESIGNER** `js/designer.js` | **Tab** | node graph · GP · **primary weapon** · secondary · palette · presets · import/export |
| 3 | **THE BAY** `js/bay.js` | **B** | hull frame · **socket radius → class/weapon/traverse/arc** · castings · GP |
| 4 | **THE FLEET** `js/fleet.js` | in flight | cycles 20 preset hulls live, overwriting your design |

### Where they actually contradict each other

- ⛔ **Two owners of hull size.** The menu's hull style sets `coreRadius`
  (22/26/31). The designer and the bay set the node array.
  `applyIdentity()` applies `hull.coreRadius` **and then** `applyDesign()` —
  so which one you see is **order-dependent**, not authored.
- ⛔ **Two owners of "what gun is this."** The designer sets one
  `primaryWeapon` for the whole ship. `hardpoints` derives a weapon **per mount,
  from its radius**. The engine now reads
  `(m.turret && m.turret.weapon) || this.player.primaryWeapon` — so the
  designer's weapon tab is a **silent fallback that almost never fires**. It is a
  control that looks live and mostly is not.
- ⛔ **Two owners of the art.** The menu picks a `packRenderer` skin for the
  whole ship; the bay picks a casting per socket. Neither knows about the other.
- ⚠ **Four rooms, one ship, and no room is the ship's home.** The menu asks
  before you have a reason to care. The fleet cycler overwrites. The designer
  speaks a vocabulary (organelles, GP, gene points) that the dreadnought turn
  left behind. Only the bay speaks mounts, traverse and arcs — the language the
  game is actually about.

## PART 3 — THE PATH FORWARD: ONE ROOM

**The bay becomes the ship's home. The menu becomes an oath. The designer
retires into the bay.** Four steps, each independently shippable, in this order.

### STEP 1 — *Witness what exists.* 15 min, Mac. **Blocks everything.**
Launch it. Fly a sector. Press **B**. Nine changes are unwitnessed, and steps
2–4 are all cheap to build and expensive to build wrong.

### STEP 2 — *The menu stops being a ship editor.* ~½ session
Keep at the title only what is a **once, at the start, identity oath**:
**callsign + crew.** Both are things you swear to, and crew is a real modifier
(`gpScale`, `contactResist`).
Move **hull style** and **skin** out — they are fitting decisions and you cannot

### STEP 3 — *The designer folds into the bay.* ~1 session

as a tab: **secondary ability · palette · presets · import/export**.

feature is a regression wearing a cleanup's clothes.
⭐ While moving the weapon tab, **make the fallback visible or remove it**: a
control that only matters when the real system is absent should say so.

### STEP 4 — *The fleet cycler tells the truth.* ~1 hour

stashes (`_stashOnce`); it just never says so.

**End state:** one room you customize in, one screen you launch from, one live

## THE SCALE RULING — 2026-08-17, 3:11pm

> *"the ship needs a far larger ratio, the weapons need a adjust size to the
> model ... a titan can not be as big as a frigate and a citadel etc. now can we
> please solve this zoom out more, its better to see a big world with a small
> ship even if its huge."* — Mac, with a screenshot

### 🔴 THE DEFECT, MEASURED FROM HIS SHOT

Across the whole authored roster:

| | grew | factor |
|---|---|---|
| the **core** | 18 → 46 px | **2.5×** |
| the **guns** | 6 → 38 px | **6.3×** |

And `sprites.js` drew every part at a **flat** `radius × 2 × 1.85`. So the
bigger the ship, the more its own armament ate it: on the TITAN a 36px siege
mount drew **133px tall against an 84px core** — the gun was 1.6× the body. The
hull could not keep up with what was bolted to it, and that is the pile of
barrels in the screenshot.

⭐ **A flat multiplier cannot express "big on a corvette, normal on a titan",
because that is a ratio to the HULL, not to the node.** That one sentence is the
whole bug.

### THE FOUR CHANGES

| # | file | change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `js/fleet.js` | **the scale ladder** — a `scale` field per hull, multiplying **layout and core radius only**, baked into the data once at parse time |
| 2 | `js/sprites.js` | **the gain taper** — `gainFor(r)` runs ×2.40 at a 6px needle down to ×1.10 at a 38px lance, so drawn size *flattens* as radius climbs |
| 3 | `js/feel.js` | **zoom 0.9 → 0.5**, floor 0.30 → 0.22, `FEEL_VERSION` 3 so the new default actually reaches his machine |
| 4 | `js/designer.js` | **the fit** — see below |

Smallest-to-largest hull reach was **5.2×**. It is now **15×**.

### ⛔ THE TRAP THE SCALE LADDER EXISTS TO AVOID

**It must never touch a cannon, pod, shield, spike or thruster radius.**
`mountBand(radius)` picks the *weapon*, traverse is `1/radius`, and `costOfNode`
prices off radius. Scaling a hull "up" by scaling every node would silently
re-band every gun in the game — a 14px LIGHT on a 1.7× hull becomes a 24px
HEAVY — and nine ships would change what they fire without one line of balance
being edited. **Layout and core only.**

### ⛔ THE OTHER TRAP: THE CAMERA MUST NOT COMPENSATE

The obvious "fix" is zoom out for capitals, in for frigates. **Do that and every
ship occupies the same fraction of the screen, which destroys the exact thing
Mac asked for.** A titan only reads as huge because the frigate beside it reads
as small *at the same zoom*. So zoom is fixed and low, and scale is told by
ships against each other and against the sector — never by how much screen one
ship fills.

### ⚠ CONSEQUENCE STATED, NOT HIDDEN

Because node radii do not grow, a capital's collision circles now sit further
apart — **a small ship can fly between a titan's hardpoints.** That may be
excellent (gaps in a capital are real, and "get inside the arc" is the keystone
of this game) or it may read as a colander. Only Mac can say.

### THE ROSTER CUT — 20 → 9

> *"get rid of most of the pre made ship designs."*

**Gone (11):** skiff, corvette, gunship, escort, marauder, cruiser, heavyCruiser,
carrier, monitor, battlecruiser, flagship.
**Left (9):** interceptor · frigate · lanceCutter · superBattleFrigate ·
destroyer · battleship · dreadnought · titan · leviathan.

Every deleted hull was a **variation, not a capability** — none carried a mount
band, class band or ability a survivor does not also carry, which is why cutting
them costs the game nothing. ⚖ That is the standing "retire nothing until its
replacement carries every feature" rule being *satisfied*, not waived. What is
left is a ladder with nine clean rungs spanning POINT→SIEGE and FRIGATE→TITAN.

### 🔴 THE DESIGNER FIT — A DEFECT MADE WORSE BY THE ABOVE

> *"the design tab is munted right now."*

`designer.js` drew the ship **1:1 into a fixed 340×340 canvas with no fit code
of any kind.** Already wrong for the largest hull (346px into 340px); after the
scale ladder, wrong for everything above a destroyer.

⭐⭐ **The fix had a trap in it worth recording: the click handler was 1:1 too.**
Scaling the paint without scaling the hit-test would have made the tool *look*
right and place every socket in the wrong hole, silently — far worse than a ship
too big to see. So `fitScale()` is one function and both the draw and
`handleBuilderClick` divide by exactly it.

### ⏭ STILL OPEN AFTER THIS

- 🔴 **FPS 7** in his shot, down from 17. On a capital the ship renders roughly
  **three full passes** — chassis strokes, then the procedural membrane underlay
  at 0.82 alpha, then 27 sprites — plus a second `drawImage` per cannon while
  firing. **This needs its own pass and the scale work does not fix it.** The
  measurement he can make in three keypresses: **G** sprites, **H** membrane,
  **J** chassis, off one at a time, watching FPS.
- ⚠ The starting chassis at `engine.js:73-76` is core 26 + one 12px cannon + two
  14px pods — **100px across, already smaller than the frigate.** It needs no
  change; at zoom 0.5 it draws ~50 screen px.

## PART 4 — THE ECONOMY: GOLD, LOOT, MARKET

> *"we have a fuck ton of modules citadels done and i can make some npc bases and
> extra shipps +modules now ... we will have enouhg from drones, citadels, bases,
> many ship classes, lots of modules, so we jut need a gold, loot drop, and
> economy, market setup. i want to do this surgically not incrementally with
> 1000 checks."* — Mac, 2026-08-17

**He is right that the content is there and the loop is not.** This is the
design, whole, so it can be built in one pass rather than discovered in fifty.

### ⭐⭐ THE ONE IDEA THAT MAKES THIS SMALL: THE LOOT TABLE ALREADY EXISTS

`art/manifest.json` is the loot table. It has **268 measured entries**, each
already carrying the two fields a drop needs — `type` (what socket it fits) and
`mountR` / `box` (how big it is, which is what picks its band). `sprites.byType`
is **already sorted small→large**.

⛔ **So do not author a loot table.** A hand-written table would be a second
copy of the library that drifts from it the first time Mac generates art. The
drop is a manifest entry; rarity is its position in the bucket it already sits
in. **Every asset he makes from now on becomes lootable the moment the manifest
is rebuilt, with no code change.** That is the difference between one session
and twenty.

### ⭐⭐ THE COLLECTION LAW — MAC, 2026-08-17

> *"we have a huge amount of drones and small enemy ships, all need naming, as
> all ships in game will be obtainable somehow — as it means low level frigs you
> vs at start will still be sick to collect later with our scaling laws."*

**Every ship in the game is obtainable.** Not a subset, not a shop tier — if it
flies at you, you can eventually fly it.

⭐ **This is what makes the scale ladder pay off twice.** The frigate that
kills you in the first hour is not disposable content, it is a thing you will
own later — and because ships are now genuinely 15× apart in size, keeping a
starter frigate in your hangar next to a leviathan is *visibly* a collection
rather than a list. The size ratio stopped being a rendering detail the moment
ownership became the goal.

**What it demands of the design:**

- ⛔ **Enemies and player ships must be ONE entity kind.** The moment an enemy
  frigate is a different shape of data from a player frigate, "obtainable" needs
  a converter, and the converter is the split roster. They are the same
  `{dx, dy, radius, type}` node graph already — keep it that way.
- ⛔ **Names are Mac's, and there is one roster.** Drones and small enemy ships
  need naming; those names come from the art packs he authors, never from a
  second list living in code. `fleet.js` supplies the **frame** (a chassis
  spec), `packs/*.json` supplies the **name**.
- ⭐ **Acquisition is the loot roll, one tier up.** A killed ship already drops
  castings from the manifest; a rare roll drops **the hull itself**. No second
  system — a hull is a node graph, and the manifest already indexes 37 `ship`
  entries that nothing currently draws.

### 🔧 THE SHIP EXPORTER — MAC WILL AUTHOR THE ROSTER

> *"ill premake the ships eventually and just export them with a tool youll put
> in there."*

This is why cutting the roster to nine was right: **the nine are a ladder to
measure against, not the fleet.** The fleet is his.

The tool is a **third tab in the bay**, not a new page — same reasoning as the
market. It needs to do exactly three things:

1. **Build** — place nodes, set radius per socket, choose the casting per socket
   (the bay already does all three).
2. **Export** — emit the hull as JSON in the `FLEET_HULLS` shape: `id`, `frame`,
   `mark`, `scale`, `color`, `primaryWeapon`, `secAbility`, `nodes[]`. That is a
   `JSON.stringify` and a copy button.
3. **Import** — paste it back and fly it.

⭐ **The export format must be the file format.** If the tool emits anything
that has to be hand-massaged before it drops into `fleet.js`, the tool has
created a second ship format and the roster will split. Export exactly what the
file already eats.

### 🛰 THE CITADEL OVERLAY — CONFIRMED ON SCREEN

> *"G is amazing bro, that view is sick — it shows me this citadel system will
> work with lay over."*

**G** toggles sprites off and leaves the procedural renderer. Mac has judged
that view good, and read the right thing off it: the procedural layer is the
**overlay** a citadel is drawn in — structure, rings, docking geometry — with
painted art bolted on top, exactly as `sprites.js` already layers chassis →
membrane → castings. So a citadel is not a new renderer. **It is the layering
the game already does, at a larger radius.** That is one less thing to build.

### THE TWO CURRENCIES, AND THE LINE BETWEEN THEM

| | what it is | drops from | spent on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MASS** *(exists)* | what your ship is **made of** → `getTotalGP()` | any kill | **capacity** — sockets, in the bay |
| **GOLD** *(new)* | what you **trade** | citadels, NPC bases, elites | **things** — castings and hulls, at market |

⛔ **GOLD MUST NOT BUY SOCKETS.** The moment it does, mass is pointless and
there is one currency wearing two names. **Mass buys body, gold buys inventory.**
That single line is what keeps this an economy instead of a bigger number.

### THE LOOP

```
raid a citadel / kill an elite
        ↓  gold + a casting drops (a real manifest entry)
dock at a friendly citadel
        ↓  MARKET: sell what you cannot fit, buy what you want
open the bay (B)
        ↓  fit it — mount radius picks the weapon, traverse is 1/radius
push into a higher danger level
        ↓  better rolls, because rarity reads the danger level
```

Every arrow lands on something that already exists.

### WHERE EACH PIECE PLUGS IN — NOTHING HERE IS FROM SCRATCH

| piece | plugs into | what is actually new |
|---|---|---|
| **gold drops** | `progression.bubbleDrops` + `BubbleDrop` | a second drop kind through the same magnet and collector |
| **casting drops** | `art/manifest.json` + `sprites.byType` | a weighted roll, ~20 lines |
| **inventory** | `saveState.doc` | one array of filenames |
| **citadels / NPC bases** | `js/sectors.js` landmark kinds (already: wreck, beacon, reef, cache) | two more kinds, one of them hostile |
| **the market** | ⭐ **a tab in the bay**, not a new screen | a list, a price, two buttons |
| **prices** | `fleet.costOfNode(type, radius)` — the GP curve that already exists | a gold multiplier |
| **rarity** | bucket position × `chamber.dangerLevel` | a roll |

⭐ **THE MARKET IS A BAY TAB AND THAT IS NOT A SHORTCUT.** PART 2 of this
document says four surfaces already change one ship. A fifth room to buy
modules in, separate from the room you fit them in, would be the same failure
again — and docking to shop then leaving to fit would be genuinely worse to
play. **You dock, and the bay opens with a MARKET tab lit.**

### NPC BASES AND DRONES — WHAT THEY ACTUALLY ARE

- An **NPC base** is a hostile citadel: the same entity, a different faction, with
  turrets. Killing it is a raid with a guaranteed casting; docking is not
  offered. ⭐ One entity kind covers both, so friendly and hostile stations can
  never drift apart.
- **Drones** already half-exist (`engine.drones`, drawn and updated each frame).
  They are a *module effect*, not a new system: a pod-class casting that spawns
  one. That keeps them inside the fitting economy instead of beside it.
- ⚠ **Citadels, bases and drones need a bucket that does not exist yet.** They
  are new **entity kinds**, not new node types — do not file them as `cannon` or
  `pod` to make them load. That is exactly the mistake that filed eight whole
  ships as `core` and chained them into the caterpillar.

### THE SURGICAL BUILD — ONE FILE, ONE SESSION, ONE WITNESS

**`js/economy.js`** — self-owning, in the idiom `feel.js` / `hardpoints.js` /
`sprites.js` / `sectors.js` already use: owns gold, inventory, the roll, and the
market's data. **Zero `engine.js` edits.** It needs exactly three seams, all of
which are wraps of functions that already exist:

1. `progression.spawnDropsFromEnemy` → also roll gold and a casting
2. `sectorField` → two new landmark kinds, one dockable
3. `bay` → a MARKET tab reading `economy.stock()`

Then **one** witness run at the end covering the whole loop: kill → drop →
collect → dock → buy → fit → the fitted part appears on the ship. **Not a check
per step.** If the loop closes once, it works; if it does not, one of three
seams is wrong and there are only three.

**Cost: ~1 focused session.** It is the single highest-value session left,
because it is the one that turns the content he already has into a game.

### 🔴 THE ONE DECISION ONLY MAC CAN MAKE

**Does dying cost you your fittings?** Everything above works either way, and it
is the difference between two different games:

- **No** — a builder. You accumulate; death costs time. Safer, gentler.
- **Yes, partially** — a run. Loot has weight because you can lose it. Sharper,
  and the reason "get inside the arc" ever feels frightening.

⛔ **I will not pick this for him.** It is the tone of the whole game, and
`progression.js:202` already halves mass on death — so there is a precedent, but
a precedent is not a ruling.

## 12b — ⭐⭐ THE WEAPON SIZE RATIO — THE ARCHITECTURE, NOT THE FEATURE

**The crux, and the reason this cannot be a raw radius edit.** A cannon node's
`radius` currently drives **four** separate things at once:

| what | where | consequence of a resize |
|---|---|---|
| how big it draws | `sprites.js` / `shaders.js` | ✅ what Mac wants |
| **which weapon it fires** | `fleet.mountBand(r)` → `hardpoints.js:99,112` | 🔴 the gun changes TYPE |
| **its firing arc** | `hardpoints.js:105` `arcA - r*arcB` | 🔴 arc silently changes |
| **its traverse — THE KEYSTONE STAT** | `hardpoints.js:106` `traverseBase*(ref/r)` | 🔴 the whole feel changes |

So "resize it to your liking" as written would silently re-band, re-arc and
re-traverse every gun he touches. This is the **same failure already documented
for `applyScale`**, which is why that function is forbidden from touching a
cannon radius. Do not re-open that hole from the other side.

### THE FIX: SPLIT THE NUMBER IN TWO

- `n.radius` — the **CLASS** radius. Keeps driving `mountBand` / arc / traverse.
  Changes only when Mac deliberately changes the gun's class.
- `n.sizeRatio` — **NEW**, default `1`. A pure **VISUAL** multiplier. Drives draw
  size and sprite scale **only**.

⛔ **THE ONE LAW:** `hardpoints.js` must never read `sizeRatio`. Grep-testable in
the witness — if it reads it, a resize can re-band a gun and the split is void.

Then his two asks both fall out of that one field:
- *"a bunch of premade size ratios"* → a preset row in the Shipyard, e.g.
  `0.75 / 0.9 / 1.0 / 1.15 / 1.35`.
- *"or a global one for each node size"* → a per-mount-class default table
  (POINT/LIGHT/MEDIUM/HEAVY/SIEGE), dialled once, inherited by every gun of that
  class, overridable per node.

⚠ **DO NOT DUPLICATE THE HULL SCALE THAT ALREADY EXISTS.** `d.scale` in
`shipyard.js:504,527,839,1003` and `hull.scale||1` in `fleet.js:137` are a
**hull-level** scale and are existing truth. `sizeRatio` is **node-level** and is
genuinely NEW. Two levels, one each — not a third mirror.
